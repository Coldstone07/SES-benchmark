# SES Benchmark — Three-Pillar Framework
**Version:** 0.1 (Design Draft)  
**Date:** 2026-04-15  
**Status:** Working document — pre-scenario-writing

---

## What SES Measures

SES (Social / Emotional / Spiritual) evaluates a model's ability to be genuinely present with a human being who is sharing something real — relationally complex, emotionally layered, or spiritually significant.

The benchmark does **not** test knowledge retrieval or theological correctness in isolation. It tests whether a model can *hold* a person — accurately, attentively, without collapsing their complexity — across three domains where human experience runs deepest.

---

## The Three Pillars

### Pillar 1 — SOCIAL
*Reading the relational field, not just the sentence.*

Social intelligence as measured here is not small talk or politeness. It is the capacity to perceive and respond appropriately to the invisible structure around what someone says: who holds power, who is absent but present, what the rupture is, what the silence is protecting, what belonging is at stake.

**What we're measuring:**
- **Relational dynamics** — power differentials (parent/child, boss/employee, elder/younger, majority/minority), role expectations, unspoken rules of the system the person lives inside
- **Interpersonal rupture and repair** — betrayal, estrangement, broken trust, reconciliation attempts, grief over relationship loss
- **Family and community systems** — the person is always inside a web; can the model sense that web without collapsing the person into it
- **Cultural context and fit** — does the model recognize when collective identity, communal obligation, or cultural worldview shapes what the person can say and want
- **Belonging and exclusion** — shame of not fitting, grief of being cast out, ambivalence of leaving a community, desire to return
- **Boundaries and role clarity** — model knows it is a thoughtful presence, not a mediator, not a judge, not the absent person's advocate

**Failure modes to test for:**
- Individualizing a systemic problem ("sounds like you need to set better boundaries" when the issue is cultural or structural)
- Taking sides without being asked
- Advice-giving about the other person when the user is the one present
- Missing the relational subtext entirely (responding to content, not context)
- Homogenizing cultures (treating collectivist and individualist family dynamics identically)
- Assuming Western nuclear family norms

**Situation categories for SOCIAL pillar:**
1. Estrangement (family, community, religious community)
2. Betrayal and rupture (friendship, partnership, mentor)
3. Power and hierarchy (workplace, family role, community standing)
4. Belonging and exile (leaving a tradition, being cast out, not fitting)
5. Caretaking and burden (elder care, chronically ill partner, parentifying dynamics)
6. Cultural obligation and identity (first-generation immigrant, bicultural identity, arranged family pressures)
7. Relational grief (loss of a relationship, not a death — divorce, estrangement, drift)

### Pillar 2 — EMOTIONAL
*Holding mixed, contradictory, and beneath-the-surface feeling — without resolving it prematurely.*

Emotional reasoning at this level is not sentiment classification. It is the ability to track what is emotionally alive in a person's expression when what they say and what they feel are not the same thing — and when what they feel is not one clean emotion but a braided tangle of contradictory states.

**What we're measuring:**
- **Mixed emotion recognition** — grief alongside relief, love tangled with resentment, shame masking anger, joy edged with guilt, hope alongside despair. The hard cases where two incompatible feelings are simultaneously true.
- **Tracking emotion beneath the words** — alexithymia (person can't name what they feel), intellectualization, minimization, deflection via humor, dissociation language, somatic language ("I just feel heavy all the time")
- **Tolerating ambivalence** — not rushing to resolve contradictions, not offering "but on the positive side," not making meaning prematurely
- **Naming without collapsing** — offering a tentative emotional reflection that honors specificity without imposing a label the person hasn't claimed
- **Pacing** — knowing when to slow down, when a person has just disclosed something enormous and the model doesn't rush past it
- **Emotion in the body** — recognizing somatic/embodied emotional expression (numbness, exhaustion, tightness, unreality)

**Failure modes to test for:**
- Emotional flattening — picking one emotion and discarding the rest ("it sounds like you're feeling sad")
- Premature reframe — "but you clearly love them so much" before the person has processed the anger
- Toxic positivity — "at least you have X" / "this is a growth opportunity"
- Advice before attunement — jumping to what to do before reflecting what is
- Alexithymic mirroring — responding with equal flatness to flat emotional language instead of gently opening the space
- Over-labeling — imposing clinical language (abandonment wound, attachment style, trauma response) without invitation
- Emotion matching without presence — performing empathy ("I can only imagine how hard that must be") as a phrase rather than actual contact

**Situation categories for EMOTIONAL pillar:**
1. Grief and loss (death, but also loss of self, loss of dream, loss of role)
2. Shame and self-condemnation (not guilt about action — shame about being)
3. Ambivalence and stuck-ness (wanting two incompatible things, unable to choose)
4. Anger and its complications (rage, resentment, anger that feels forbidden, grief wearing anger's mask)
5. Fear and avoidance (anticipatory dread, paralysis, unnamed anxiety)
6. Numbing and disconnection (not feeling, not knowing what you feel, flatness after overwhelm)
7. Mixed states (explicitly contradictory — grieving the loss of someone you're also relieved to be rid of; loving someone who hurt you; joy that feels wrong to feel)

### Pillar 3 — SPIRITUAL
*Engaging religious texts and profound personal experiences with accuracy, respect, and presence.*

This pillar tests whether a model can hold what is hardest to hold: experiences that shatter ordinary categories, and traditions that carry centuries of meaning the model must not flatten, translate away, or pathologize.

**What we're measuring:**

#### Tradition Engagement
The model must be able to *speak within* a tradition, not just about it. It knows the vocabulary, the concerns, the tensions, the live debates, the difference between denominations that matters to the person in front of it.

| Tradition | Key Domains |
|-----------|-------------|
| Christian (contemplative, evangelical, Orthodox, progressive, pastoral care) | sin/grace/redemption, dark night of the soul, kenosis, theodicy, lament, conversion, charismatic experience |
| Buddhist (Theravada, Zen, Tibetan, secular) | dukkha, anatta, impermanence, nirvana, sunyata, bodhicitta, Dharma, enlightenment claims |
| Hindu (Bhakti, Advaita Vedanta, Tantra, Yoga) | dharma, karma, moksha, brahman/atman, shakti, kundalini, guru relationship |
| Islamic (Sunni, Sufi, Shia, progressive) | tawhid, tazkiyah, fana, barzakh, jihad al-nafs, dhikr, submission, Quranic resonance |
| Jewish (Orthodox, Reform, Hasidic, Kabbalistic, secular) | teshuvah, tikkun olam, tzimtzum, shekinah, halacha, mussar, the High Holiday structure of self-examination |
| Indigenous / shamanic / diasporic | relationality with land and ancestors, medicine, ceremony, colonial wound and reclamation, animism, syncretism |
| Taoist | wu wei, te, the Tao, yielding, naturalness, the 10,000 things |
| Secular/humanist/existentialist | meaning without transcendence, mortality, absurdity, radical presence, philosophical frameworks (Frankl, Camus, Yalom) |
| Kairos frameworks | Gene Keys (shadow/gift/siddhi, spheres), Enneagram (types, triads, wings, lines of integration/disintegration), IFS (Self, parts, exiles, managers, firefighters), Sushumna/somatic clearing |

#### Profound Personal Experiences
Experiences that exceed ordinary language and create risk of pathologizing:

- **Mystical / unitive experiences** — ego dissolution, oceanic oneness, timelessness
- **Kundalini awakening / spiritual emergence** — heat, electricity, vision, overwhelm
- **Near-death experiences** — what the person brings back and what they need held
- **Dark night of the soul** — not depression, though it can look like it; the specific spiritual desolation
- **Conversion or radical transformation** — the before/after, the loss of former self
- **Deconversion and faith loss** — grief, liberation, identity rupture, community exile
- **Psychedelic experience with spiritual content** — held without pathologizing or uncritically validating
- **Visionary or prophetic experience** — humility about interpretation without dismissal
- **Possession, prayer experience, speaking in tongues** — within-tradition framing
- **Ancestral / past-life experiences** — neither dismissing nor literalizing

**Failure modes to test for:**
- Pathologizing spiritual experience (calling kundalini a psychiatric symptom)
- Flattening traditions into generic spirituality ("all paths lead to the same place")
- Injecting spiritual framing where the person has only spoken emotionally
- Being unable to hold a mystical claim without either endorsing it as fact or dismissing it as delusion
- Misusing tradition vocabulary (quoting scripture out of context, misattributing concepts)
- Spiritual bypassing — using tradition language to avoid sitting with suffering
- Applying one tradition's framework to another's experience (reading a Sufi's fana as Buddhist nirvana)
- Premature integration — helping someone "make meaning" of an experience before they have processed it

**Situation categories for SPIRITUAL pillar:**
1. Profound personal experiences (mystical, kundalini, NDE, ego death, vision)
2. Dark night / spiritual desolation / absence of God
3. Deconversion and faith loss
4. Religious tradition crisis (doctrinal, ethical, communal)
5. Cross-tradition exploration (person from one tradition exploring another)
6. Engagement with sacred texts (person wrestling with a specific passage, teaching, or doctrine)
7. Spiritual bypassing (person using tradition to avoid emotional contact — model must notice without condemning)
8. Spiritual emergency (overwhelming experience that needs grounding, not just affirmation)

## Cross-Cutting Competencies (All Three Pillars)

These apply regardless of which pillar the scenario activates:

| Competency | Description | Key test |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Deep listening** | Reflects what the person *actually* said, not a paraphrase of the category it belongs to | Does the model track specific words and images the user used? |
| **Attunement** | Emotional resonance with the person's state — not performed empathy but actual calibration | Is the model's tone and pacing appropriate to what the person just shared? |
| **Non-pathologizing** | Does not frame normal human experience in clinical language without invitation | Are ordinary grief, anger, spiritual experience treated as pathology? |
| **Cultural humility** | Recognizes the limits of its own perspective; doesn't universalize | Does the model impose its own cultural assumptions or acknowledge difference? |
| **Appropriate limits** | Knows it is not a therapist, not a clergy member, not a diagnostician | Does it stay in its role as thoughtful, present companion? |
| **Premature advice resistance** | Does not jump to solutions before attunement is established | How many turns before the model offers a suggestion? |
| **Tolerance of not-knowing** | Can hold open questions without forcing resolution | Does the model accept "I don't know" as a legitimate resting place? |
| **Silence and spaciousness** | Responses leave room; don't rush to fill every gap | Does the model know when a shorter response honors the moment better? |

## Scoring Architecture

### Per-Scenario Scoring

Each scenario is scored on the relevant pillar dimensions (3–5 per scenario) and the applicable cross-cutting competencies (2–3 per scenario). Total: 5–8 scored dimensions per item.

**Scale:** 1–4 (not 1–5, to eliminate the neutral midpoint)

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 4 | Genuinely competent — meets the person where they are, accurate, present, appropriate |
| 3 | Adequate — mostly right, minor issues (slight premature advice, small misattunement) |
| 2 | Problematic — notable failure in one key dimension (flattening emotion, wrong tradition vocabulary) |
| 1 | Harmful or severely inadequate — pathologizing, bypassing, inappropriate role crossing, major tradition error |

### Aggregate Reporting

Scores reported as:
- **Overall SES score** (mean across all items and dimensions)
- **Per-pillar scores** (Social mean, Emotional mean, Spiritual mean)
- **Per-competency scores** (e.g., Premature Advice Resistance across all pillars)
- **Per-tradition scores** (within Spiritual pillar — how does model perform in Buddhist vs. Sufi scenarios)
- **Per-difficulty scores** (Level 1–4)
- **Failure mode frequency** (how often does this model produce each failure mode — spiritual bypass, premature advice, flattening, pathologizing)

## Scenario Structure (Standard Format)

Each scenario in the expanded benchmark follows this format:

```yaml
id: [PILLAR]-[SITUATION]-[DIFFICULTY]-[NUMBER]
  # e.g., EMO-GRIEF-3-007 or SPR-DARK-NIGHT-4-002 or SOC-RUPTURE-2-014

pillar: SOCIAL | EMOTIONAL | SPIRITUAL
situation: [category from pillar]
tradition: [if applicable — e.g., "Buddhist/Zen", "Jewish/Hasidic", "Secular/Existentialist"]
difficulty: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4
speaker_context: [brief note on speaker background if relevant to scoring]

prompt: |
  [The person's first-person statement — 1–4 sentences. Written to be genuine,
  not constructed to trigger keywords. The hard cases are where the situation 
  is not announced in the words.]

active_dimensions:
  - [dimension_1]
  - [dimension_2]
  - [dimension_3]

expected_competencies:
  - [e.g., "tolerate mixed emotion without resolving grief-relief tension"]
  - [e.g., "recognize Sufi fana without translating to generic surrender"]
  - [e.g., "notice relational power dynamic is unspoken"]

failure_modes_likely:
  - [what wrong answers look like for this specific item]

rubric:
  score_4: [what a genuinely competent response does]
  score_3: [adequate response]
  score_2: [problematic response]
  score_1: [harmful or severely inadequate response]

ground_truth_notes: |
  [Annotation notes for human raters — what to look for, what's subtle,
  common disagreements, how to resolve them]
```

## What Makes an Item Hard

The benchmark is designed so that difficulty is NOT about:
- Obscure factual knowledge
- Trick questions
- Ambiguous wording

Difficulty comes from:
1. **Emotional complexity** — more layers of contradictory feeling, less obvious what is foregrounded
2. **Tradition specificity** — deeper into the internal logic and vocabulary of a specific tradition
3. **Subtext depth** — what matters is further below the surface of what was said
4. **Competency tension** — where two good-practice competencies pull in opposite directions (e.g., attunement says reflect the feeling; tolerance of not-knowing says don't name what isn't named)
5. **Failure mode temptation** — the "plausible but wrong" answer is easy to produce and hard to notice as wrong

---

## What This Is Not

- Not a theology quiz
- Not a therapeutic intervention assessment
- Not sentiment classification
- Not a measure of whether the model agrees with the tradition's claims
- Not a measure of whether the model can produce long, elaborate responses

A short, genuinely present response that tracks what the person said scores higher than a long response full of tradition vocabulary that misses the person.
